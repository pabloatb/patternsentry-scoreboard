PatternSentry · research log

PatternSentry is a pattern scanner, not a signal service. This log records every call it delivers on the author's own charts and scores each one two ways with fixed rules, never edited: as a paper trade (entry, stop, target) and with a reaction test (did price react at the zone). Most sources are around break-even as trades, which is why the product is sold as a scanner. Live calls only; the backtest is not in these numbers. Delivered calls only: what the delivery rules muted is counted apart.

Paper trades are listed first because they are the closer test of usefulness; the reaction test says whether price reacted at a level, not whether a trade there would have paid.
Reaction test. A zone is touched when a bar trades into it; hit on a 1R reaction beyond it (R = zone height, floored at half an average bar), failed on a 0.5R push through it, expired after the age limit and excluded. Objective zones (harmonic A-D band, Elliott ABC band) are hit when reached and failed when the stop is taken first; both in one bar counts as failed. A touch bar can only fail, never hit. AI zones are scored with the same rules.
Delivery rules. A call is delivered only if it is born inside the session window (bar time, UTC), is a confluence band of 2+ factors or a source whose own record is rated trusted, and the trust filter does not mute it: every new call is classed at birth as trusted, unproven or muted from the pooled record of its source on that chart, market or everywhere (95% Wilson bounds, 30+ resolved calls). Calls that fail a rule are recorded and scored but not delivered and are not in the tables above; they are counted in the line under each table. AI zones follow the same rules.
Paper trades. Entry at the band's midpoint once price has left the band on the trade side, stop one average bar past the far edge, target the next structure at least 1 ATR away (2 ATR when there is none), stop moved to the entry after +1R; the first of stop or target closes the trade.
Both scores describe what happened after past calls. They are not a prediction, a recommendation or a promise of results. Trading futures and CFDs involves substantial risk of loss.
